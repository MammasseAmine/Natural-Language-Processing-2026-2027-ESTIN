Introduction to NLP

Teaching machines to understand human language
What is NLP?

Natural Language Processing (NLP) is a field that helps computers understand, work with, and even create human language. It is also called Computational Linguistics, Human Language Technology, or Natural Language Engineering. NLP sits at the meeting point of Linguistics, Computer Science, and Artificial Intelligence / Machine Learning.

NLP = turning messy human language into something a computer can understand and use.

Human Language vs Computer Language

Human Language

  • Can be unclear – words have many meanings
  • Meaning changes with context and tone
  • Many ways to say the same thing
  • Uses idioms, metaphors, and slang

Computer Language

  • Clear and exact – no double meanings
  • Follows strict rules and structure
  • One instruction = one action
  • Designed by people, not evolved naturally
Why NLP Matters

Language is Everywhere

We use language all the time. NLP helps machines talk to us in our own words.

Too Much Text

Every day we create huge amounts of text (messages, articles, posts). NLP helps sort and understand it.

Talking to Machines

Chatbots, voice assistants, and smart speakers all need NLP to understand and reply.

The Challenges

Ambiguity – the biggest problem

Lexical ambiguity: "I went to the bank." (Did you mean a river bank or a money bank?)
Syntactic ambiguity: "I saw the man with the telescope."

Idioms & Metaphors

"It's raining cats and dogs." – A computer might think animals are falling from the sky. NLP must learn the real meaning: heavy rain.

Context & Hidden Meaning

"The trophy didn't fit in the suitcase because it was too big." – We know "it" means the trophy, because suitcases don't shrink. Computers need to learn that kind of world knowledge.

Key Takeaway

NLP turns the rich, messy beauty of human language into something a computer can use. The hardest parts come from what makes language human – unclear meanings, context, and creativity. Teaching a machine to really understand words is the goal.
