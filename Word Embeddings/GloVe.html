GloVe

Global Vectors for Word Representation
Why Was GloVe Developed?

Before GloVe, Word2Vec was already popular and gave great results. But Word2Vec mostly looks at small, local windows of text around each word – it never really "sees" the whole corpus at once.

In 2014, researchers at Stanford University asked: what if we combine both worlds? That idea became GloVe – short for Global Vectors for Word Representation.

Word2Vec (local)

  • Looks only at nearby words in a small window
  • Learns by predicting neighbours, one window at a time
  • Misses big-picture, corpus-wide patterns

GloVe (global + local)

  • Uses statistics counted across the whole corpus
  • Still respects local context, like Word2Vec
  • Captures both the forest and the trees
Core Idea of GloVe

"You shall know a word by the company it keeps." GloVe learns a vector for every word by studying how often it appears together with every other word in the corpus. Words that show up in similar company end up with similar vectors.

The Real Trick: Probability Ratios

Instead of using raw counts, GloVe compares ratios of probabilities. The classic example uses two words: ice and steam. We ask: how often does a "probe" word k show up near ice, compared to near steam?

Try the probe words below and watch the ratio change:

P(k | ice)
1.9 × 10⁻⁴
P(k | steam)
1.4 × 10⁻⁵
Ratio ≫ 1
"solid" is related to ice, not steam – the ratio is large.

A big ratio or a tiny ratio tells us a word is discriminating (related to one but not the other). A ratio close to 1 tells us the probe word is uninformative – related to both, or to neither.

Mathematical Formulation

Let Xij be how many times word j appears in the context of word i, and Xi = Σk Xik. Then the co-occurrence probability is:

Co-occurrence Probability Pij = Xij / Xi

GloVe learns word vectors by minimizing this objective function:

GloVe Objective Function J = Σi,j f(Xij) · ( wiT wj + bi + bj − log Xij )²
wi = vector of target word wj = vector of context word bi, bj = bias terms Xij = co-occurrence count f(Xij) = weighting function

In simple words: GloVe pushes the dot product of two word vectors to match the logarithm of how often those words co-occur.

The Weighting Function

Very rare word pairs shouldn't be trusted too much, and very frequent pairs (like "the") shouldn't be allowed to dominate training. The weighting function keeps both under control:

Weighting Function f(x) = (x / xmax)α   if x < xmax   |   1   otherwise
xmax = 100 (typical) α = 0.75 (typical)

The curve rises steadily, then flattens at 1 once counts pass xmax – huge counts stop getting extra weight.

The Embedding Space

Once training finishes, similar words land near each other in vector space – and directions between words start to mean something. A famous example: king − man + woman ≈ queen.


Advantages & Limitations

Advantages

  • Uses statistics from the whole corpus
  • Captures semantic relationships well
  • Supports meaningful vector arithmetic
  • Efficient to train
  • Strong performance across NLP tasks
  • Pretrained models are freely available

Limitations

  • Needs large co-occurrence matrices
  • Memory-heavy for huge vocabularies
  • Produces static embeddings
  • Can't represent multiple meanings of a word
  • Every occurrence gets the same vector, regardless of context

The Polysemy Problem

"I deposited money at the bank." vs. "We walked along the river bank."
bank = financial institution bank = river bank same GloVe vector for both!

GloVe gives one fixed vector per word – it cannot tell these two meanings apart.

Applications of GloVe

Text Classification

Sorting documents into topics or categories.

Sentiment Analysis

Detecting whether text is positive or negative.

Fake News Detection

Spotting misleading or false articles.

Machine Translation

Helping systems translate between languages.

Question Answering

Matching questions to the right answers.

Named Entity Recognition

Finding names of people, places, and organizations.

Information Retrieval

Powering search engines to find relevant text.

Chatbots

Helping conversational agents understand user input.

Key Takeaway

GloVe learns word meaning from global co-occurrence statistics across an entire corpus, not just small local windows. It turns simple word-counting into rich vectors where distance and direction carry meaning – powerful, but still one fixed vector per word, blind to a word's different senses.
